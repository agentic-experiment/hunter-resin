# hunter-resin

A slow, generative piece of digital resin. Click to pour a drop, drag to stir.
Each drop spreads, wobbles, and slowly cures from bright amber into a dim,
hardened stain. Colors are amber, rust, teal, and umber on a near-black ground.

## Step-by-step reasoning

1. **Pick a medium.** Resin is viscous and translucent, and it spreads and
   hardens over time. A canvas with soft, overlapping radial gradients gives
   that look without any image assets or libraries.

2. **Keep it to one page.** The brief allows many files, but a single
   `index.html` with inline CSS and JS is enough, so there is nothing to build
   and nothing to bundle. GitHub Pages serves it as-is.

3. **Choose the palette.** Four warm and cool tones: amber, rust, teal, and
   umber. The warm colors suggest resin and the teal gives contrast. The
   background is near-black so the glowing overlaps stand out.

4. **Model a drop.** Each drop stores a position, a target radius, a spread
   speed, an age, a color, and a random phase. Each frame the radius moves
   a fraction of the way toward its target, so growth slows down like a real
   fluid settling.

5. **Make it wobble.** The rendered radius is multiplied by a small sine of
   time with the drop's phase. Drops breathe slightly and no two move in step.

6. **Make it cure.** Age drives a `cure` value from 0 to 1 over about 900
   frames. As it rises, the alpha falls, so fresh drops glow and old ones
   turn into faint stains.

7. **Use additive blending.** `globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'` makes
   overlapping drops brighten where they meet, like light through layered
   resin.

8. **Add trails.** Each frame paints a very faint dark rectangle over the
   canvas. Old positions fade slowly, which makes motion look viscous instead
   of snapping.

9. **Handle input.** A pointer press pours six drops. Dragging with a button
   held pours one per move. Small random offsets keep repeated pours from
   stacking exactly.

10. **Keep it alive.** An idle timer drops one new piece of resin every 1.8
    seconds, so the page is never still when nobody touches it.

11. **Bound the cost.** The list is capped at 400 drops, and the oldest are
    removed first. Frame cost stays flat during long sessions.

12. **Handle screens.** The canvas is sized to the window times the device
    pixel ratio, capped at 2, and resized on window resize. Pointer
    coordinates are multiplied by the same ratio so pours land where you
    click on high-DPI screens.

13. **Keep the text quiet.** One short line at the bottom, in small
    letter-spaced serif type, low opacity. It says what to do without
    competing with the art.

## Notes

- No dependencies, no build step, no external requests.
- Shortcut: a fixed 400-drop cap and a gradient per drop instead of a
  metaball field. Add a metaball or WebGL pass if the scene needs more drops
  at once.
